Raphinha: The Footballer Who Almost Gave Up on His Dream

Repeated rejection and pressure nearly led Raphinha away from football. With family support and psychological care, he began to find confidence and a sense of self beyond the game.

10/18/265 min readJonathan Wang

Raphinha playing for FC Barcelona. Image taken from Fox Sports.

Editorial Note

Stories Beyond the Game

This series highlights athletes whose stories remind us that success and mental health are not mutually exclusive. Behind every medal, championship, and victory is a person navigating challenges that often remain unseen.

Raphinha is largely considered one of the best footballers currently, leading FC Barcelona as their captain and breaking goal-scoring records in the current La Liga season. Many fans, however, don’t know that there was a time when Raphinha had contemplated ending his dream because of repeated rejections.

Raphael Dias Belloli, known professionally as Raphinha, was born in Porto Alegre, Brazil. He grew up in Restinga, a working-class neighborhood that is widely characterized as suffering from significant economic hardship and high crime rates. His family suffered from this, struggling financially and having very limited opportunities for economic and educational advancement.

Raphinha’s mother, Liz, worked in daycare cafeterias while his father, Rafael, worked as a musician. Despite this constant pressure, his family continued to reinforce that Raphinha should live a good and honest life, emphasizing the importance of humility, discipline, and respect.

In a 2022 interview with La Vanguardia, Raphinha explained that although his family lacked material wealth, his family made up for it and he lived in a family with very strong morals.

For Raphinha and other families like his, football was not simply an extracurricular activity or a sport just for fun; it was an opportunity to escape poverty and lift their families out of the slums.

Poverty would be a huge obstacle in Raphinha’s journey of becoming a professional footballer. He described experiencing severe hunger and having to ask for food from strangers. He also witnessed his peers being pressured to turn to a life of crime because of their limited opportunities. Some of his childhood friends would become drug traffickers and others would pass away in this life of crime.

Football was legitimately Raphinha’s lifeline out of this life and offered him the opportunity to get out of a dangerous life.

Passion could only get Raphinha so far when every other child was in a similar situation as he was, scraping to change their lives. Raphinha struggled to establish himself in professional youth academies and was rejected numerous times from major Brazilian clubs. Concerns about his small stature and physique contributed to rejection after rejection.

Raphinha watched as other young players secured their spot in the top youth academies while he struggled and grasped at straws. Eventually, he would continue his journey through smaller clubs, trying to establish himself as an athlete that people wanted to take a shot on.

When Raphinha was approximately 16 years old and playing for Audax, he would suffer a serious ankle injury. Rather than worrying about him, Raphinha’s head coach criticized him for his swollen ankle. The coach questioned whether he was truly injured and suggested that he was only occupying a spot that could have gone to another player.

All of this would culminate in Raphinha considering quitting football at the age of 17. Without football, Raphinha would very likely be forced to find an entry-level job while making up for the education he missed out on while chasing his dream. His father encouraged him to reconsider.

Then, when he turned 18, Raphinha thought of quitting once again. Injuries and struggling to establish himself ate at his motivation and he felt like abandoning the dream that he once so strongly grasped for.

His mother urged him to reconsider and think carefully about what abandoning a dream he had dedicated his whole life to would lead him to lose. His mother’s decision to speak out and his family’s constant support pushed Raphinha to push on, and, without them, Raphinha might have never become the player he is today.

After overcoming the multitude of difficulties he faced in Brazilian youth football, Raphinha finally got his opportunity to continue his career in Portugal, joining Vitória de Guimarães in 2016.

His performances would eventually lead to him being signed to Sporting CP and subsequently moving to Rennes in France. Although his career was finally progressing the way he hoped, each move to a different country meant new cultures, languages, and people that he had to experience and learn. These transitions affected him mentally and brought enormous expectations from fans with each move.

Finally, Raphinha would join Leeds United in the Premier League in 2020. He would develop into one of England’s most important attacking players and become a key contributor to Leeds’ efforts to battle relegation during their difficult 2021–22 season.

In 2022, Raphinha’s long-lasting dream of playing for FC Barcelona would finally come true, and he would be signed at just 25 years old, reaching one of the highest levels of professional football.

However, his story wouldn’t end here. Moving from Leeds to Barcelona meant enormous expectations from fans trying to see the justification in the transfer. However, adapting to Barcelona proved more difficult than expected, and Raphinha struggled to reproduce the magic he had at Leeds.

Raphinha frequently faced competition for playing time and, as an athlete who had struggled to make it to the world stage, finally being able to climb it and not getting a break was incredibly draining for him. Raphinha admitted considering leaving the club because of his performance and the backlash he was receiving.

Raphinha openly discussed the mental consequences he endured as a professional footballer and there were many times that he struggled to get up the next morning to train. He considered quitting football and pursuing a different career path many times, even after all the hard work and effort he had put in to get where he is today.

Raphinha would seek psychological help during this hard time, later emphasizing that he believed that athletes should really have access to psychologists and criticizing the notion that just because they were athletes meant that they had to deal with the psychological burden themselves.

His psychologist helped him understand his identity beyond football and that his worth did not just come from the sport that he played, helping to really address his lifelong conflict of quitting or continuing what was his dream.

Paired with Barcelona manager Hansi Flick’s support and reassurance that Raphinha should stay and that everything was going to be all right, Raphinha began regaining his confidence and his attacking contributions would increase dramatically.

As his performances improved, Raphinha would become the captain. A player once battling to stay in the club and considering quitting multiple times became the leader who exemplified the qualities that he had previously overlooked in himself.

Raphinha achieved the dream he had pursued since childhood, yet his biggest win wasn’t playing on the biggest stage in the world, but discovering himself outside the sport he had dedicated his life to pursuing.
