Jude Bellingham: From England's Scapegoat to the Nation's Hope

Jude Victor William Bellingham went from being treated as England’s wonderboy to feeling like the national team’s scapegoat. Through an arduous journey, Bellingham had to rediscover his enjoyment of representing England on the biggest world stages and demonstrates how a public perception of being confident and mature can cause people to overlook your vulnerability.

07/25/266 min readJonathan Wang

Editorial Note

Stories Beyond the Game

This series highlights athletes whose stories remind us that success and mental health are not mutually exclusive. Behind every medal, championship, and victory is a person navigating challenges that often remain unseen.

Jude Victor William Bellingham was born on June 29, 2003, in Stourbridge, England. He is the eldest son of Mark and Denise Bellingham, with a younger brother, Jobe, who would also become a professional footballer, playing for Dortmund.

Football was embedded in the family before Jude came to be who he is today. Mark Bellingham worked as a police officer but was also a prolific non-league striker. Jude would grow up around someone who understood the joy of scoring and the discipline required to become a professional footballer.

The family was incredibly supportive of the two sons’ dreams. Mark would become involved in managing Jude’s professional interests, while Denise ensured that Jude had a stable head on his shoulders and managed his transition abroad. When Jude moved to Dortmund as a teenager, she accompanied him to Germany and helped him maintain a stable home environment.

This structure shaped Jude’s personality. His confidence emerged from the discipline, adult responsibility, and care his parents showed him. This allowed him to grow up to become a humble and professional footballer.

Bellingham would be repeatedly treated as exceptional when he was a teenager, playing against grown professionals when only 16 years old and moving countries to play the sport he loved. At 19, he was the captain of Borussia Dortmund, and at 20, he would transfer to Real Madrid, becoming their starting midfielder.

For the media, Jude was very mature and carried himself confidently, leading observers to frequently treat him as older than he truly was. This became consequential to the criticism he would face later on in life.

“For the media, Jude was very mature and carried himself confidently, leading observers to frequently treat him as older than he truly was.”

His record-breaking transfer to Dortmund when he was 17 years old accelerated his emotional development, as he was no longer being protected by his youth academy, Birmingham City. Now, he was competing in title races, Champions League matches, and hostile stadiums with aggressive rivalries.

Jude would perform exceptionally, only fueling the already high expectations fans had placed on him. By June 2024, he was a Champions League winner, La Liga Player of the Season, and the face of Adidas. England was looking for a player who could bring titles back home, and they were looking at Jude.

At Euro 2024, England struggled even after topping their group, performing poorly compared to previous matches. Media criticism questioned the starters, especially Bellingham. Despite having one of the most promising young squads in England’s history, they weren’t performing like the fans expected them to.

Bellingham would score a crucial goal against Slovakia, helping England evade elimination. England was able to reach the final because of his bicycle kick, which would be considered one of the best goals scored in England’s history. He would be England’s hero until they eventually lost to Spain in the final.

Instead of remembering his amazing performance, many discussions focused on why England didn’t bring it home, and all of these questions were directed toward the biggest star of the squad. Jude would later explain feeling scapegoated and losing his passion for representing his national team because he didn’t feel appreciated. Bellingham revealed the racial abuse his family faced, his grandmother being too afraid to leave home, and reporters appearing around his family. He explained that people accused him of refusing media interviews because they believed he thought he was above them.

Even after the previous disappointment faced in 2024, Bellingham would embrace the responsibility once again during the World Cup, becoming one of England’s best players in the tournament. They would have an amazing run in the tournament, placing third overall among 48 teams.

Instead of booing, England supporters sang “Hey Jude,”

showing him that they were with him. Two years earlier, he had been blamed by the entire country; however, now he had tens of thousands of supporters singing his name.

Through the ups and downs, Jude’s character never faltered. He continued to be respectful, humble, and take up the responsibility meant for him. He never forgot where he came from, the people who allowed him to be in the position he was in today, and he never allowed anyone to change him. Jude would embrace his character through the moments when he was scapegoated and the times when England supporters would serenade him, never forgetting who he was despite the backlash he had faced before.
