Son Heung Min's Story

Behind Son Heung-Min’s rise from a demanding childhood foundation to global football stardom is a life shaped by expectation. His story shows how discipline, humility, and emotional resilience can carry someone through pressure that never truly disappears.

08/10/264 min readJonathan Wang

Son Heung Min. Image taken from BBC

Editorial Note

Stories Beyond the Game

This series highlights athletes whose stories remind us that success and mental health are not mutually exclusive. Behind every medal, championship, and victory is a person navigating challenges that often remain unseen.

Son Heung-Min is one of the most prolific footballers in the world and a national treasure in South Korea. However, just like everyone else, he faced enormous pressures and hardships to get to the place he is in today, and even today, faces the exact same pressures. Son struggled to carry the extraordinary expectations of his father, his country, and himself, while trying to maintain humility, joy, and emotional intelligence. His story emphasizes how discipline becomes resilience rather than resentment for what allowed him to succeed.

Son was born in the Gangwon Province of South Korea. His father had been a professional footballer for Sangmu FC before suffering a knee injury that led to an early career retirement. Son’s father was not obsessed with living the perfect career through Son, but he was addicted to realizing a perfect football foundation. Son’s father would later call this the Son Football Academy based on this philosophy.

Son’s father believed that one should master football before competing and playing in games. This belief would guide Son’s early training. Son spent his youth repeating basic skills. Rather than seeing football as recreational and something to enjoy, Son saw it as a structured, repetitive, and demanding sport from an early age. He would admit later on that his training was incredibly difficult and exhausting as a child; however, he has never once criticized his father’s role in this. He has consistently resisted this characterization and said that those years built the technical foundation later needed for him to compete at the highest level.

At 16, Son would move from South Korea to Germany to join Hamburger SV’s academy. He would move to a different country, have to learn a new language, be engulfed in a different culture, and practice in a completely different football philosophy. Son had little familiarity with Germany and made few friends.

Making it into professional football was very difficult, especially as an Asian player. Few Asian attacking players had made it to European Leagues, so Son’s entrance into this club was a very unique experience for many players. He had to prove that he belonged and could thrive in this environment.

When Son signed for Tottenham Hotspur for 22 million pounds, expectations were enormous for him to succeed. His first season was very underwhelming, and the media portrayed that he struggled to adapt to the physicality, the football system, and the language and cultural adjustments. Son later admitted that he considered returning to Germany after the first season; however, his manager convinced him to stay and see it through.

As Son succeeded and thrived at Tottenham, he would slowly become South Korea’s symbol. Expectations stopped belonging to only him, and he started representing the ability and potential of South Korea in every World Cup, Asia Cup, and international qualifying match. Millions of fans expected Son to deliver, and few understood the enormous responsibility he had to bear on his shoulders.

Additionally, for years, one question followed Son during the prime of his career: would he have to leave European football to complete mandatory military service for his country?

South Korea required most able-bodied men to complete military service, and for elite footballers, an interruption of eighteen months can dramatically alter a career.

This pressure reached new heights during the 2018 Asian Cup, when winning gold would mean he could be exempt from his service. South Korea would defeat Japan in the final, and when the whistle blew, Son fell to tears. Years of uncertainty about whether or not he would need to leave football disappeared in one night.

“Sometimes, hardship is waking up every day with enormous expectations that feel like they will never disappear.”

Because Son was one of South Korea’s biggest stars, public scrutiny and criticism were expected. Every drip in form became national news. Son would experience this pressure for more than a decade, yet he never responded with frustration but rather redirected the praise to his teammates while accepting the criticism himself.

Although online stories have framed Son’s father as abusive and controlling to get Son to where he is today, Son doesn’t support what any of them say. Son has repeatedly expressed his gratitude to his father, emphasizing that without him, none of the success he achieved would have been possible.

Son’s story exemplifies that hardship is not always dramatic or visible. Sometimes, hardship is waking up every day with enormous expectations that feel like they will never disappear. For over two decades, Son carried those expectations while remaining known as one of football’s kindest and most respectful. The quiet emotional resilience is what solidifies him as one of the most inspirational athletes in the world.
